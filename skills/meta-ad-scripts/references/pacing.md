# Length, pacing, and Meta platform benchmarks

## Word counts by duration

Natural spoken pace runs ~2.5–3 words per second (~125–170 words/minute depending on energy level — short-form ad delivery tends to run at the faster end).

| Duration | Word count target | Structural implication |
|---|---|---|
| 15s | ~30–40 words | No room for a real "body." Hook + one proof point/beat + CTA. Best matched to Testimonial, Before/After (single beat), or a one-line Mythbust. |
| 30s | ~70–90 words | The standard length. Room for hook → brief problem → solution/mechanism → one concrete proof point → CTA. Most structures in `structures.md` are naturally 30s-shaped. |
| 60s | ~150–180 words | Room for a real arc — full Testimonial or storytime with a beginning/middle/end, Day-in-the-Life, a multi-beat Mythbust, or a Harmon Brothers-style comedic build. Extra length is budget for the middle, not a slower opening — the hook still has to land in the first 1–3 seconds. |

Leave a little headroom below the max word count (aim for the script to read a beat faster than the hard duration limit) so natural emphasis and pauses fit without rushing.

## Meta-specific benchmarks (Feed / Reels / Stories)

- **Feed**: browsing mindset, users scrolling quickly — value proposition needs to be obvious immediately. Best video length 15–30s.
- **Reels/Stories**: full-screen, fast binary decision (watch vs. skip) — hook needs to be even more front-loaded than Feed. Best length 6–15s.
- **In-stream**: more runway available, 15–120s.
- Very short videos (1–9s) actually had the *lowest* hold rate in one dataset — there is a floor: long enough to deliver one complete idea, not just a fragment.

## Hook rate / hold rate — what they mean for scripting

- **Hook rate** = 3-second views ÷ impressions. ~20–25% is solid for most accounts, 30–50% reachable with optimized creative, 35–45%+ considered elite.
- **Hold rate** = of those who passed 3 seconds, how many kept watching. Roughly half of viewers who pass 3 seconds will watch to 30 seconds when the opening lands.
- If hook rate is high but hold rate is low (<~25%), that's a **pacing problem in the body**, not a hook problem — the fix is tightening the middle and adding another small interrupt a few seconds in, not rewriting the opening line again. Practically: don't let any single beat run more than 2-3 sentences before giving the viewer a new reason to keep listening (a new fact, a turn, a specific number, a shift in what's being promised).

## CTA placement

Primary CTA goes in the final 3–5 seconds, stated as one concrete next action (a verb + a specific outcome), not a vague nudge. For 15–30s spoken scripts there usually isn't room for more than the one closing CTA line; for 60s scripts a soft, brief internal nudge mid-script plus a clear final CTA is reasonable, but don't force a second CTA if it costs a sentence that isn't otherwise earning its place.
