# Script structures — catalogue and selection guide

All structures below are distilled from `research-notes.md`. Use this file to pick the right shape for the specific product, audience, and goal — never default to the same structure every time.

## How to choose

Ask three questions before picking a structure:

1. **Audience awareness** — has this audience already heard the standard pitch for this category, or are they hearing the problem framed for the first time?
   - Unaware / cold → the problem needs to be named for them. Use **Hook-Problem-Solution-Proof-CTA**, **PAS**, or **Mythbusting**.
   - Warm / skeptical (already tried competitors or the "obvious" fix) → they need a reason the standard answer failed them. Use **Contrarian/Hot Take**, **Tested Alternatives**, or **Testimonial/Skeptic**.
2. **What's provable on camera** — does the product have a visually obvious before/after, or is the win more about a feeling, a routine, or a number?
   - Visual transformation → **Before/After** or **BAB**.
   - Numeric/business result (SaaS, coaching, B2B) → **Hook-Problem-Solution-Proof-CTA**, **WHY/WHAT/HOW**, or **Question → Twist → Reveal**.
   - Lifestyle fit → **Day-in-the-Life**.
3. **Goal of the ad** — hard direct-response conversion, or brand/retention/top-of-funnel?
   - Hard conversion → PAS, Hook-Problem-Solution-Proof-CTA, Tested Alternatives, Contrarian.
   - Brand-building / warm retargeting / retention → Day-in-the-Life, Testimonial, Harmon Brothers-style comedic sell.

Never pick a structure just because it's the default one you know — the mismatch is exactly what makes scripts generic. State to yourself which of the three questions above drove the choice before writing.

## The structures

### 1. Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA
The workhorse. Name the hook, name the problem in one line, introduce the product as the fix, give one concrete specific result, close with one clear action.
Best for: cold audiences, direct-response goals, most physical products and SaaS tools.

### 2. PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solution)
State the problem, make it worse/more real for one beat (the cost of not fixing it, the thing people don't admit), then resolve it with the product.
Best for: pain points people underestimate or normalize ("you probably think this is just how it is").

### 3. BAB (Before → After → Bridge)
Life before the product → life after → the product is the bridge between the two.
Best for: transformation-led categories (visual or lifestyle), and works well when "after" is something the audience actively fantasizes about.

### 4. WHY/WHAT/HOW
Open on the problem via a curiosity or contrarian angle → agitate the pain the audience has been quietly tolerating → pivot to the mechanism (what makes this solution different) and tease it without fully explaining it → resolve in the CTA.
Best for: products/services with a genuinely different mechanism worth being curious about (a specific method, a different ingredient, a different process) — SaaS and coaching offers often fit here well.

### 5. Tested Alternatives / Skeptic
"I tried [X, Y, Z]" → quick, honest dismissal of what didn't work and why → introduce this product with the specific reason it's different → one concrete result → direct CTA.
Best for: warm audiences who've already tried the obvious fixes; categories with lots of mediocre competitors.

### 6. Mythbusting
Name a false belief the audience holds → debunk it with a fact, a number, or a credible source (not just an opinion) → reposition the product as the belief-correct answer.
Best for: categories where the audience is working off outdated or wrong assumptions. Keep the tone informative, not smug — the goal is to make the viewer feel smarter, not scolded.

### 7. Contrarian / Hot Take
"Why [common practice] is [wrong/costing you]" — take a real, defensible position against the standard advice in the category, back it with proof, then land the product as the implication of being right.
Best for: skeptical, previously-pitched audiences; categories crowded with near-identical claims. Needs genuine substance — a hot take without proof reads as a gimmick.

### 8. Question → Twist → Reveal
Open on a pattern-interrupting question, surprising visual cue described in words, or bold claim → build tension by withholding the explanation for a beat (cut on the question, not the answer) → deliver the payoff once curiosity has built.
Best for: products with a genuinely surprising result or mechanism worth the delayed reveal.

### 9. Testimonial
A customer (voice) speaking directly about their own experience, doubt, and result.
Best for: handling skepticism directly through lived experience rather than claims; strong for 15–30s formats.

### 10. Day-in-the-Life
The product is woven naturally into a routine; nobody is "selling" anything, the value shows up as one moment inside a bigger story.
Best for: warm audiences, brand-building/retention goals, lifestyle products. Keep under ~45 seconds — it stops working past that in paid placement.

### 11. "They don't want you to know" / Secret Angle
"What [authority/industry] doesn't want you to know about [topic]" — opens a curiosity gap and positions the viewer as about to get insider information.
Best for: categories with a credible "the establishment/industry has an incentive to hide this" angle. Needs to be genuinely true or defensible — don't manufacture a conspiracy that doesn't hold up.

### 12. Harmon Brothers-style comedic sell
Solid direct-response sales structure (a real problem, a real mechanism, a real CTA) delivered through one character/spokesperson with comedy woven through — humor supports the sale, it isn't the point of the video.
Best for: categories that can carry personality and can afford a longer, more produced-feeling script (typically 45–60s); avoid if the brand/product doesn't support a strong character voice.

Do not chase "funny" or "viral" as a goal in itself — the structure only works if the underlying sales logic is sound; the comedy is delivery, not substance.
