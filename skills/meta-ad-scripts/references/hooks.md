# Hook formulas — catalogue

Hooks decide most of an ad's fate: research found hooks account for 60–80% of performance variance, and hook rewrites can swing performance 50–200% while body-copy changes rarely move more than 20–30%. Spend the most creative effort here, and always deliver 3 hooks from genuinely different angles — never 3 phrasings of the same idea.

Pick ONE mechanism per hook. Don't blend a question with a stat with a bold claim in the same opening line — it dilutes the interrupt.

## Match hook type to audience state

- **Question** — audience recognizes the problem the moment they hear it named. Best for problem-aware audiences; questions get people mentally answering before they consciously decide to keep watching.
- **Stat / shocking number** — audience is skeptical or analytical and needs proof of scale before they'll care. Best when you have a real, specific number.
- **Counterintuitive / belief-flip statement** — audience thinks they already know the answer. Needed when you're up against an entrenched "common wisdom."
- **Pain-first** — audience is living the frustration but hasn't named it yet. Say it back to them more precisely than they'd say it themselves.
- **Outcome-first** — audience is motivated by the destination more than the problem. Lead with the specific result.

## Hook formula list (with example patterns)

1. **Big claim** — "[$specific outcome] in [timeframe]." e.g. "€10.000 omzet in één weekend."
2. **Fast claim** — a big claim compressed into an even shorter, punchier line. e.g. "Verdubbel je resultaat. Vannacht nog."
3. **Authority** — "Ik heb [large number/investment/experience] gedaan, en dit is wat niemand je vertelt." e.g. "Ik heb voor 2 miljoen euro aan ads gedraaid."
4. **Before/After** — "[state 1] → [state 2]." e.g. "Van 40 minuten wachtrij naar 4 minuten."
5. **Rival compare** — direct, named or implied comparison to the standard alternative. e.g. "3x sneller dan wat je nu gebruikt."
6. **No-limits / objection-preempt** — remove the obvious excuse up front. e.g. "Ook als je nog nooit een advertentie hebt gemaakt."
7. **Question** — a question the viewer can't help but answer in their head. e.g. "Waarom werkt jouw ad niet, terwijl die van je concurrent wel scoort?"
8. **Info / curiosity gap** — promises information without giving it away. e.g. "Er is één ding dat elke winnende ad gemeen heeft."
9. **Newness** — leads with what's new/different/just changed. e.g. "Sinds deze update werkt de oude aanpak niet meer."
10. **Exclusivity** — frames the viewer as getting access others don't have. e.g. "Dit vertellen we normaal alleen aan klanten."
11. **Belief flip** — "Je denkt dat [common belief]. Dat klopt niet." e.g. "Je denkt dat een duurdere ad beter werkt. Het tegendeel is waar."
12. **Solution callout** — names the fix before the problem, banking on the audience already knowing the pain. e.g. "Dit is de enige reden dat mensen na 3 dagen stoppen met [dieet/routine]."
13. **Direct callout** — names the exact audience so the right person self-selects instantly. e.g. "Ben je zzp'er en factureer je nog met Excel?"
14. **"They don't want you to know" / secret angle** — "Wat [autoriteit/industrie] niet wil dat je weet over [onderwerp]." Opens a curiosity gap and casts the viewer as about to get insider info. Needs to hold up under scrutiny — don't invent a conspiracy that isn't real.
15. **Mythbusting hook** — states a common false belief the audience holds, ready to be debunked in the next line. e.g. "De meeste mensen denken dat [wijdverbreide aanname]. Dat is fout."
16. **Tested-alternatives hook** — "Ik heb [X, Y, Z] geprobeerd" — signals lived, skeptical experience before the pitch starts.

## Delivery matters as much as the words

Hook wording alone doesn't guarantee performance — the energy behind the delivery is what actually transmits the emotion that drives the click, not just the words on the page (a recurring point from Meta ad creative strategists). Since this skill only produces spoken text, write hooks that are easy to deliver with natural, punchy energy: short clauses, a clear stress point, nothing that requires a performance to rescue a clunky sentence.

## Timing

Get to the interrupt immediately — hooks landing in well under a second reportedly outperform 2-3 second "intros" by 2-3x. Never open with a greeting, a brand name, or a throat-clearing sentence ("Hey guys," "Wist je dat...", "Vandaag wil ik het hebben over...") — that IS the intro that kills hook rate.
